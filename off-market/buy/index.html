How to buy an off-market condo in San Francisco

To buy an off-market condo in San Francisco, join The San Francisco List: it is free, and members see every Exclusive Deal with its address, price and photos, investor-owned units on The Exchange, and the private prices owners have named for units that are not for sale. You can also make an offer on any unit in any building, listed or not, and Tim McMullen delivers it to the owner of record.

Buying off-market, in five steps

  1. Join The San Francisco List

    Free, with an email. Members see each Exclusive Deal’s address, price and photos, and get an email the moment a new one arrives.

  2. Watch the buildings you want

    Search off-market in a specific building: every building has its own page with its recorded sales, HOA record and every unit. Watch a building and hear about every new listing and owner’s price in it.

  3. Make an offer on any unit

    Pick a unit, listed or not, set your price and terms, and read the letter before anything is sent. Tim delivers it to the owner of record and tells you if they answer.

  4. Read the HOA record first

    Ask for the disclosures and the HOA review on any building: dues, reserves, assessments, litigation and insurance, from the association’s own documents.

  5. Close like any purchase

    An off-market purchase closes like any other: a written contract, the HOA documents, inspections, escrow and title. Tim represents you through it.

Buying off-market in San Francisco: questions

What is an off-market condo?
A condo that can change hands without a public listing, so it is not on Zillow, Redfin or the other listing sites. On Condo Market SF that means Exclusive Deals shown only to members of The San Francisco List, investor-owned units on The Exchange, and the private prices owners have named for units that are not for sale.
How do I find off-market condos in a specific San Francisco building?
Open the building’s page on Condo Market SF and watch it. Members hear about every Exclusive Deal, new listing and owner’s private price in the buildings they watch, and can write to the owner of any unit in it.
Can I buy a condo that isn’t for sale?
You can make an offer on one. Every unit on the public record has a page. Name your price and terms and Tim delivers your letter to the owner of record. Many owners will not sell; some will answer.
Do I still get the HOA documents off-market?
Yes. California requires a condo seller to give the buyer the association’s documents (Civil Code §4525) whether or not the unit was listed. Members can also read the HOA reviews already on file for a building.
Is it free?
Yes. Membership of The San Francisco List, watching buildings and writing to an owner are all free.
