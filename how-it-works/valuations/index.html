How it works · Valuations

What a condo is worth, and who is saying so.

Three different things get called a valuation. They are not the same, they do not carry the same weight, and on this site each one says which it is on its first line. Here is the difference.

01. Automated valuation

Arithmetic. A program picks recorded sales in the building and nearby that match on size, bedrooms, floor and date, works out a range per square foot and applies it to the unit. Nobody has looked at the unit. It cannot see condition, a remodel, the view, the stack, the HOA's finances, or what is pending but not yet recorded. The estimates on the big portals are this kind of number, and so are the automated valuations this site prepares on request.

What it is not: an opinion of value, an appraisal, or advice on what to list or offer at. No licence stands behind it and no one is accountable for it.

02. Broker’s opinion of value

A judgement, signed. Tim McMullen, a licensed California real estate broker (CA DRE #02016832), chooses the comparables by hand — the same stack, the same floor plan, the sales that actually compare — adjusts for what the records cannot see, weighs what is pending and listed right now, and puts a name and a licence on the number. In California, saying what a specific home is worth is a licensed activity, and this is the licensed version. On this site it is called a Comp Report once Tim has signed it, and it is the number to take into an offer, a listing decision or a negotiation.

What it is not: an appraisal. A broker’s opinion is an agent’s professional judgement, not a regulated report.

03. Appraisal

A regulated report. Prepared by a state-licensed or certified appraiser to professional standards, usually because a lender, a court or an estate requires one. Neither of the numbers above replaces it.

How you get one: your lender orders it when you finance a purchase or a refinance; for a probate, a divorce or a tax matter, an attorney or an accountant will say when one is needed. Tim can tell you what to expect but does not prepare appraisals.

We do not publish an estimated value on any building page or unit record. The only numbers attached to a specific unit live on their own private pages, and each one says, in the first line, which of the three it is. The full sourcing is on the methodology page.

A number with a name on it is a different product.

Portal estimates are everywhere and they are free because nobody is accountable for them. A broker’s opinion of value is signed, and the person who signed it will stand in a negotiation behind it. We keep the two apart so that you always know which one you are reading, and so that the free arithmetic on this site is never mistaken for the licensed judgement it is not.
