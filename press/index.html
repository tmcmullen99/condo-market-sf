The story

A San Francisco condominium is worth about $1.3 million and is one of the least liquid assets a person can own. Not because buyers are scarce — because the act of finding out what someone will pay costs the owner months and tens of thousands of dollars, spent up front, with no guarantee of a sale.

To list conventionally you move out, paint, stage, and carry the mortgage and HOA dues while the unit sits — often while renting somewhere else at the same time. If it doesn't sell, that money is gone and a stale price history follows the unit for years.

So most owners never find out. Last year 471 of those 12,183 units changed hands: 3.9% turnover. The other 96% weren't unavailable. They were never asked.

San Francisco Condo Market inverts the sequence. It publishes the full recorded sale history of every tracked building, free and without an account, and then lets a buyer submit a written offer on any unit in any building — listed or not. A buyer can send as many as they like. An owner receives a real number on the home they already live in, and simply decides.

An unsolicited inquiry asks an owner to do work. An offer asks an owner to make a decision. That difference is the entire product.

Tim McMullen, founder

Story angles

Four separate stories live here. Most journalists take one.

◆ The category

The buyer moves first

Every off-market product to date has been seller-initiated. Zillow's Make Me Move needed an owner to post a price; iBuyers need an owner to request one; pocket-listing networks need a signed agreement. Here the owner has done nothing at all — and still gets an offer.

◆ The economics

Selling costs more than commission

Moving out, painting, staging, carrying two housing costs and absorbing the risk of no sale. That's the real barrier to liquidity, and it's paid before a single buyer walks in. Owners here skip all of it, and carry a $10,000 credit against commission.

◆ The market

Prices up, volume flat

SF condos are the one Bay Area segment that never recovered from 2020. In 2026 they posted the sharpest single-year move in the modern dataset — while transaction volume went nowhere. Fewer trades at higher prices is a liquidity story, not a demand story.

◆ The operator

For buyers

The thing nobody tells you: this was always legal. There was simply no mechanism.

You have always been allowed to approach an owner and offer to buy their home. Almost nobody does, because there's no road — most buyers don't know it's possible, and most agents don't know how to structure the conversation with someone who wasn't thinking about selling.

On San Francisco Condo Market a buyer opens any of 142 buildings, sees every recorded sale in it, picks a unit, and submits a structured written offer. Listed or not. As many units, in as many buildings, as they want. The addressable market stops being the handful of listings on a portal and becomes the whole city.

Year over year that is +11.4% on median price and +10.8% on price per square foot — the sharpest single-year move in the modern dataset. It is also still 12% below the 2020 peak. Both halves are true, and the second one matters: San Francisco condos are the only Bay Area segment that never came back.

Volume is the quieter finding. 249 sales against 256 a year earlier — flat, while prices climbed double digits. Fewer trades at higher prices is what a market looks like when demand meets a very small pool of willing sellers.

Boilerplate

Short — 45 words

San Francisco Condo Market is a marketplace covering 142 San Francisco condominium buildings and 12,183 units, where a buyer can submit a written offer on any unit — listed or not. It publishes the recorded sale history of every tracked building free of charge.

Long — 105 words

San Francisco Condo Market is a marketplace for San Francisco condominiums built on a single observation: only about 4% of the city's condo stock trades in a given year, because selling conventionally costs an owner months and thousands of dollars before a buyer ever appears. The platform publishes the full recorded sale history of 142 buildings and 12,183 units free and without an account, then allows a buyer to submit a structured written offer on any unit, whether or not it is for sale. It is operated by McMullen Properties, founded by ten-year San Francisco condo agent Tim McMullen, CA DRE #02016832.

Founder — 55 words

Tim McMullen has spent a decade selling San Francisco condominiums across the full width of the market, from one-bedroom TICs and co-ops to full-floor residences. Methodology. Figures reflect the San Francisco Condo Market database as of July 25, 2026. Sales are recorded transactions in tracked buildings; management and association transfers are excluded, as are transactions under $100,000. Year-to-date comparisons use an identical January 1 – July 24 window in each year. Price per square foot uses sales with recorded square footage above 300 sq ft. Unit counts reflect buildings publishing a unit count; some tracked buildings do not, so total stock is understated. Turnover is trailing-twelve-month sales divided by tracked units.
