The short version

Two units, one building, and a number you should see before anyone pitches you.

Both residences sit inside The Brannan — 219, 229 and 239 Brannan Street, the three-tower Christian Liaigre building a block off the Embarcadero. That matters, because the most honest comparables for a Brannan unit are other Brannan units, and specifically the ones on the lower floors. Six one-bedrooms have traded in the building recently and three of them were below the tenth floor.

Those three cleared $888, $988 and $989 per square foot. Applied to your two floor plans that produces roughly $920,000 for #6H and $976,000 for #3E — a combined $1,896,000. Against the 2017–19 market the building is down 16.4%, which is the second-best result of the eight South Beach buildings measured here.

The part most valuations will not tell you: #6H was purchased for $1,020,000 in September 2018. The building has not recovered that level on the lower floors. This analysis is built to show you exactly where the gap is and what each of the three ways to come to market does about it.

The building

Second of eight. Only one nearby building held more.

Comparing one-bedroom price per square foot in the 2017–19 market against today, The Brannan is down 16.4%. Of the eight South Beach buildings with enough sales on both sides of that window, only 200 Brannan held more. The glass towers that define the modern skyline are down 19% to 37%.

Control for floor plan and it improves further. The building’s recent sales skew toward larger low-floor units, which drags an unmatched median down; matched line by line the decline runs roughly 7%, ranging from −16% on the small 723 sq ft B line to +5% on the 808 sq ft A line, which is worth more today than it was in 2019.

Where the honest limit of that claim is

200 Brannan is down less. Any statement that The Brannan has held value better than every nearby building is not supportable, and a buyer’s agent with the same export would find that in an afternoon. Second of eight is the accurate claim, and it is a strong one.

Three sales is a thin recent window. The Brannan’s current-era median rests on three closed one-bedrooms. It is enough to establish a level, not a trend, and it will move as more units close.

What the data does support: a mid-rise, concrete, Liaigre-designed building with a 75-foot lap pool and 24-hour staff has behaved like a different asset class from the 2015–19 glass towers. Those towers are down 19% to 37%. That is the comparison that means something to a buyer choosing between them today.

Where #3E is softer than #6H

At 1,137 square feet — confirmed against the 2002 purchase record — #3E is larger than every recent comparable in the building. The largest is 229 #2C at 1,047 square feet.

Price per foot falls as units get larger, so #3E’s figure is extrapolated one step beyond the observed range rather than interpolated inside it. That is a real limitation and it is why the range on #3E is wider than the one on #6H. The first sale of a 1,100+ square foot one-bedroom in this building will settle it; until then, treat $976,000 as the centre of a wider band.

What actually moves the number

In a condominium, the floor and the outlook are the price.

Square footage tells you what you own. Orientation, floor and view tell you what someone will pay for it. This building measures that plainly: 219 #15A on the fifteenth floor cleared $1,436 per foot with sweeping skyline and Bay Bridge views, while the lower-floor units cleared $888 to $989. Some of that gap is size and renovation. Most of it is what you see out the window.

Floor

Upper-floor Brannan one-bedrooms cleared a median $1,144/sf against $988 below the tenth floor. Both subjects are low-floor, which is already priced into the conclusion rather than discovered later by a buyer.

Outlook

The B and A lines at 219 face the skyline and the bridge. The C, G and H lines sit lower and look into the podium, the pool deck and the courtyard. Quieter and more private — a real selling point to the right buyer, and one worth writing rather than leaving to a floor number.

The building itself

Concrete construction, Christian Liaigre interiors, 24-hour attended lobby, 75-foot heated lap pool, 3,000 sq ft fitness centre, deeded parking. This is the reason the size-matched decline is 4% and not 20%.
